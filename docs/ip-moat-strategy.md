# IP Moat & Registration Strategy

This document captures the categories of intellectual property that the
auto-evolving threat-intel pipeline generates as a side-effect of running,
explains why each category is defensible, and recommends a protection and
registration path. It is intended as a strategy reference for the
operator and as a briefing document for outside IP counsel; nothing here
constitutes legal advice and every claim noted as "potentially patentable"
should be reviewed by a registered patent attorney before filing.

The strategic frame the rest of this document develops is that
Preston-Check's defensible moat is a **process moat**, not a content
moat. The detection patterns themselves are open-source by design and
will remain so — that is what gives the scanner its distribution and
community-trust position. The IP that compounds and is hard to clone is
the production system that *generates, validates, attests, and
continuously self-audits* those patterns, plus the artifacts that
production system accumulates over time: versioned corpora, signed
attestation logs, methodology specifications, and the
telemetry-quorum-trained anomaly models. Competitors with venture money
can clone the architecture in three months; they cannot clone eighteen
months of attested process output.

## Executive summary

Six categories of IP fall out of the design, each with a different
protection mechanism and a different defensibility lifetime. Listed
because they are genuinely discrete and a lawyer will need them
itemised:

1. **Process flywheel** — the compounding loop between corpora,
   telemetry, and meta-verification. Trade-secret-protected; defensible
   indefinitely while operational.
2. **Reproducible corpora** — versioned, ground-truthed datasets of
   vulnerable and benign code samples for fintech. Trade-secret-
   protected on the rolling current snapshot, copyright-and-CC-licensed
   on the published embargoed older snapshots.
3. **Attestation log** — the cryptographically-signed public record of
   every catalog change with full provenance. Copyright-protected,
   trademark-able as a named index, citation-based moat.
4. **Methodology specification** — the published standard for
   verification-walled security-catalog automation. Copyright-and-
   trademark-protected, openly licensed for adoption with retained
   trademark.
5. **Defensive patent portfolio** — three to five filings on the genuinely
   novel pieces (LLM-vs-detector adversarial loop with enforced model
   diversity, quorum-gated telemetry feedback method, continuous
   meta-verification of mechanical security gates). Filed defensively
   under an Open-Invention-Network-style pledge to prevent offensive
   enforcement.
6. **Time-to-detection benchmark** — the publicly-tracked operational
   metric ("median hours from CVE publication to detection-pattern
   shipped"). Trademark-able as a named index, marketing-and-PR moat.

The strongest moat is the flywheel (category 1) because all three other
operational categories (corpora, attestation log, telemetry models)
strengthen it and each other; cloning any one without the others is
useless, and all three must be accumulated over real operating time to
have value. The most legally-portable moats are the patents (category
5) and the trademarks attached to categories 3 and 4. The most
community-trust-building outputs are categories 3 and 4; they are also
what creates the prior-art barriers that protect category 5.

## Strategic frame: the process moat

A content moat protects what you have. A process moat protects how you
make it. Content moats are fragile in the open-source era — anyone can
fork the artefact. Process moats are durable because they require the
combination of accumulated operational data, continuously-running
infrastructure, and institutional knowledge to operate. The clearest
analogue is Stack Overflow: anyone could fork the question-and-answer
text, but nobody can fork the eighteen years of accumulated
edit history, reputation graphs, and tag taxonomies.

Preston-Check's process moat works the same way. The catalog of
detection patterns is open-source; anyone can fork it. But forking the
catalog gives a competitor only what *was* detected. The process —
multi-source ingestion, LLM synthesis, mechanical verification gates,
adversarial red-teaming, reproducible corpora, attested catalog
changes, quorum-gated telemetry feedback, continuous meta-verification
of the gates themselves — is what continuously *grows* the catalog.
Without the process, a fork stales within weeks as new CVEs are
published and the forked catalog ceases to track them.

This frame has three implications for the protection strategy. First,
keeping detection patterns open is *strategically correct* — open
patterns build community trust, drive scanner adoption, and create the
distribution that gives the SaaS layer its customer base. Closing the
patterns would shrink the addressable market and gain nothing
defensible. Second, the process components must be either trade-secret-
protected (corpus current snapshots, synthesis prompt library, gate
orchestration code) or patent-protected (the genuinely novel methods).
Third, the artefacts the process produces (attestation log, scorecards,
methodology spec) should be aggressively published, because
publication establishes priority, drives citations, and creates a
public record that itself becomes prior art against would-be cloners.

## Category 1 — Process flywheel (trade secret)

The compounding loop is: corpora improve detection accuracy, accurate
detection drives scanner adoption, scanner adoption drives opt-in
telemetry volume, telemetry volume improves the quorum gates,
quorum-gated feedback retunes the catalog and informs corpus
expansion, retuned catalog continues to detect more accurately. Each
arrow strengthens the next; each requires the previous. The flywheel
is the moat.

What is protectable as trade secret here: the synthesis prompt library
(the actual prompt templates and few-shot examples used to generate
patterns); the orchestration code that sequences ingestion, synthesis,
validation, adversarial loops, and merge; the rolling-current-snapshot
of the corpora; the quorum-anomaly-detection model parameters trained
on telemetry; and the operational logs that track gate effectiveness
over time.

Protection mechanism: keep the orchestration code and prompt library in
private repositories (not the public preston-check/preston-check repo),
hosted under the operating LLC's GitHub organisation. Encrypt the
rolling corpus snapshot in S3 with a customer-managed KMS key. Train
quorum-anomaly models inside Cloudflare Workers and never export
weights. Apply a written confidentiality policy for any contractor or
employee with access. Mark documents internally as "Confidential —
Trade Secret" so any unauthorised disclosure is unambiguously
actionable.

Defensibility lifetime: indefinite while operational, but trade
secrets evaporate the moment they are published or independently
re-derived. This is why the flywheel must be paired with the patent
portfolio (category 5) and the methodology spec (category 4) — the
patents and the spec create defensive perimeters that survive
disclosure.

## Category 2 — Reproducible corpora (trade secret + copyright + Creative Commons embargo)

The corpora are the ground-truth datasets that make the validation
loop work. They consist of two halves. The positive corpus is a
labelled collection of vulnerable code samples, harvested from
public CVE references, exploit-DB, OSV-referenced PoC repositories,
and HackerOne-disclosed bug bounty reports — each tagged with the
vulnerability class it exemplifies, the language and framework it
belongs to, and a hash of the original source. The negative corpus
is a labelled collection of vulnerable-pattern-free code samples,
drawn from popular open-source repositories under permissive licences
(GHArchive's curated subset, OSS-Fuzz's corpora, Linux distribution
package sources) — each tagged with language, framework, and
provenance.

What makes the corpora valuable IP is not the individual samples
(those are public) but the *labelling*, the *curation decisions*,
and the *combination* of positive and negative sets calibrated to
each other for fintech relevance. The same individual file in the
negative corpus is meaningless without the metadata declaring "this
is a clean Spring Boot 3.x controller, expected to produce zero
findings under any check tagged spring/java." The metadata is the IP.

Protection mechanism: dual-track. The current rolling snapshot stays
private, encrypted, accessible only to the orchestration code. Older
snapshots — embargoed for twelve months — get published under a
Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 licence with the
manifest, allowing academic use and community verification while
preventing commercial competitors from taking the latest snapshot.
The published snapshots become prior-art that establishes our
priority of curation; the private current snapshot retains the
operational moat.

Registrable IP: the corpus name and its associated trademark (e.g.,
"Preston-Check Verification Corpus — fintech edition" with a
distinctive logo). Filing class 9 (downloadable software / data) and
class 42 (computer services) at USPTO with parallel Madrid Protocol
filings for EU and UK markets.

Defensibility lifetime: trade-secret on the current snapshot
indefinitely; copyright on the database structure and curatorial
metadata for life-of-author-plus-70 (or the corporate equivalent);
trademark indefinitely with renewals.

## Category 3 — Attestation log (copyright + trademark + citation moat)

Every catalog change ships with a signed JSON attestation containing
the source CVE/advisory ID, the synthesis prompt hash, the model used,
the corpora hashes at validation time, the computed TPR/FPR, the
adversarial-loop transcript hash, the sandbox pass result, and the
gate effectiveness scores at merge time. These attestations are
publicly committed to the repository and mirrored to a public log
endpoint. Within twelve months of operation, the cumulative
attestation log becomes the most comprehensive public record of how
fintech vulnerabilities have manifested in code.

This has direct IP implications. The structured log format, the
signing schema, and the verification protocol are copyrightable
expressions. The cumulative log itself is a database covered by
database-rights regimes in jurisdictions that recognise them (notably
the EU's sui generis database right). The public-facing identifier
attached to the log can be trademarked.

Strategic value: citation moat. Once the attestation log is cited in
academic security papers, in compliance frameworks (NIST SSDF, PCI
DSS, DORA), in insurance underwriting models for cyber risk, or in
regulatory guidance, removing or replacing it becomes infeasible for
incumbents. The log embeds itself in regulatory plumbing, and
regulatory plumbing is the stickiest possible position for an IP
asset.

Registrable IP: trademark the index name (suggested: "Preston-Check
Vulnerability Attestation Index" or "PVAI" as the abbreviation,
class 9 + 42). File copyright on the schema and the verification
protocol specification. In EU jurisdictions, register the database
right separately.

Recommended branding angle: the index should be presented as a
public-good infrastructure that Preston-Check operates, not as a
proprietary asset. This positioning maximises citations and
regulatory adoption; the trademark and copyright protections kick in
only if a competitor tries to fork or rebrand the asset itself, which
is the only outcome we need to defend against.

## Category 4 — Methodology specification (copyright + trademark + open standard)

The architecture itself — wide-range threat-intel intake combined
with mechanical verification walls, continuous meta-verification of
those walls, reproducible corpora, public dual-use audit, and quorum-
gated telemetry feedback — is a methodology that applies generally to
any vertical that wants safe security-catalog automation. It applies
to medical device security, automotive (CAN bus, AUTOSAR),
industrial control (ICS/SCADA), aerospace, and to any sub-vertical
within fintech beyond the current scope.

Protection strategy: publish the methodology as a versioned open
specification under a permissive licence (Creative Commons
Attribution 4.0, allowing commercial adoption with credit). Trademark
the methodology's name (suggested: "Verified Auto-Catalog
Methodology" — VAC, or a more distinctive coined term). Encourage
adoption — every adoption generates a citation, a community of
implementers, and a network of vertical experts who anchor the
methodology to Preston-Check as the originator.

This is the IBM-quality-systems / NIST-standards play applied to
security-catalog automation. The trademark on the methodology name
becomes the licensing hook: any vertical-specific implementation that
wants to claim conformance must license the trademark, which is
where revenue accrues. The methodology itself is freely available;
the right to claim "VAC-compliant" is licensed.

Registrable IP: trademark the methodology name (class 42). Copyright
the specification text. Optionally file design rights on any visual
or diagrammatic elements (process flow charts, gate diagrams) that
are distinctive and likely to be copied.

Strategic synergy with the standalone-identity persona: a
pseudonymous operator publishing methodology specifications under
the Preston X handle, with a public record of attested catalog
changes demonstrating the methodology in production, becomes a
named authority figure in fintech security in a way that no team-
attributed corporate product can. Authority accrues to the named
individual, not to a shifting roster, and the IP and the persona
compound on each other. Filings should go through the operating LLC
to preserve the persona while creating a real legal entity that owns
the registered marks.

## Category 5 — Defensive patent portfolio

Three to five inventions in the design appear novel enough to support
patent claims in most jurisdictions. The recommendation is to file
defensively, attach an Open-Invention-Network-style non-aggression
pledge, and use the filings to prevent competitors from blocking
Preston-Check rather than to extract licence revenue. Defensive
filings remain valuable even with a non-aggression pledge because
they create prior-art that prevents anyone *else* from patenting the
same method and asserting against us.

A non-final list of candidate filings, with rough claim language —
each requires review by a registered patent attorney before filing,
and the claim language below is a starting point, not a draft:

1. **LLM-vs-detector adversarial loop with enforced model diversity.**
   A method for generating security detection patterns wherein a first
   LLM synthesises candidate patterns, a second LLM operating under
   different model weights attempts to generate evasive code samples
   that contain the target vulnerability but fail to match the
   candidate pattern, evasion successes are appended as new test
   cases to the synthesis prompt for the first LLM, and the loop
   iterates until N rounds occur without successful evasion, wherein
   the requirement that the two LLMs operate under different model
   weights ensures that evasion classes specific to a single model's
   training distribution are surfaced rather than masked.

2. **Quorum-gated telemetry feedback for security catalog
   modification.** A method for accepting or rejecting modifications
   to a deployed security detection catalog wherein telemetry signals
   from opt-in scanner installations are aggregated by a quorum
   function requiring N distinct installation fingerprints
   contributing the signal over M time-window before the signal is
   permitted to trigger a catalog modification, the fingerprints
   being derived without identifying the source installation,
   thereby preserving installation anonymity while preventing
   adversarial telemetry poisoning from manipulating catalog
   contents.

3. **Continuous meta-verification of mechanical security gates with
   public effectiveness attestation.** A method comprising one or
   more mechanical security gates that validate auto-generated
   security checks before deployment, a continuously-running
   adversarial harness that submits synthetic adversarial inputs to
   each gate and records gate response, an effectiveness score
   computed per gate per time-window from the harness output, and a
   public attestation log that records each effectiveness score
   alongside each catalog modification gated by that gate, wherein
   any drift in gate effectiveness becomes detectable and publicly
   verifiable before adversaries discover and exploit it.

4. **Reproducible corpus-based validation of security detection
   patterns.** A method for validating candidate security detection
   patterns wherein the corpora used for validation are themselves
   built reproducibly from public dataset manifests, each corpus
   build emits a deterministic hash, the hash of the corpus used for
   each validation is recorded in the attestation accompanying the
   validated pattern, and any third party can re-derive the corpus
   from the manifest and reproduce the validation, thereby providing
   independently-verifiable validation provenance for security
   detection patterns.

5. **Shadow-deploy with quorum-gated promotion for security
   detection patterns.** A method comprising deploying a candidate
   security detection pattern to opt-in scanner installations in a
   suppressed-output mode wherein findings are recorded to telemetry
   but not surfaced to the user's report, observing telemetry signals
   over a configured shadow-deploy window, applying a quorum gate to
   determine whether the pattern's field behaviour meets configured
   thresholds, and promoting the pattern from suppressed-output to
   surfaced-output only upon quorum confirmation, thereby preventing
   noisy patterns from degrading user trust before field validation
   is complete.

Recommended filing strategy: provisional applications first (USPTO
$300 each, twelve-month conversion window) to lock priority dates
inexpensively. Convert to non-provisional within twelve months,
extending to PCT for international protection. Budget approximately
USD $15,000–$30,000 per invention for full prosecution including
attorney fees through grant. Total budget for the portfolio:
$75,000–$150,000 over three to four years.

Non-aggression pledge: publish a written commitment that the patents
will not be asserted against open-source projects, against
academic research, or against good-faith implementations under a
defined-class licence. The Open Invention Network or similar
infrastructure provides standardised pledge mechanisms. The pledge
preserves community trust while retaining defensive value.

Critical timing constraint: filing must precede substantial public
disclosure of the inventions in the methodology spec, the
attestation log, or the codebase. Most jurisdictions outside the US
have strict absolute-novelty rules with no grace period. Plan to
file provisionals on all five inventions before the first commit
that ships the corresponding code. The public attestation log will
itself become prior art twelve months after first commit, so the
filing window is fixed and short.

## Category 6 — Time-to-detection benchmark (trademark + marketing moat)

Once hourly KEV ingestion is operational, the system can publish a
continuously-updated metric: median hours from CVE publication on a
threat-intel source to detection-pattern shipped in the catalog.
Competitors using human-curated catalogs are structurally incapable
of matching this number; over time the gap widens.

The metric itself is a marketing asset. The branded version of the
metric — published continuously, badged in product comparisons, cited
in case studies — becomes an IP asset in the same way that
"Underwriters Laboratories certified" is an IP asset for UL. The
trademark is on the badge and the index name, not on the underlying
measurement.

Registrable IP: trademark the index name (suggested: "PCDx —
Preston-Check Detection Index" or similar). File the badge as a
certification mark, which is a stronger and more defensible mark
type than a regular trademark when applied to performance
benchmarks.

Defensibility lifetime: indefinite with renewals. The certification-
mark route is the same legal mechanism that protects "Energy Star,"
"USDA Organic," and "ISO 9001" — well-tested, well-defended, well-
understood by courts.

## Trade secrets vs open source: the published-vs-closed split

The full split, captured in one place for legal reference:

**Closed (trade secret + copyright):**

The synthesis prompt library and few-shot example sets used to
generate detection patterns. The orchestration code (private repo
under the operating LLC) that sequences the seven loops. The
rolling-current snapshot of both corpora. The quorum-anomaly
detection model parameters trained on telemetry. Operational logs
of gate effectiveness over time. Internal incident reviews, post-
mortems, and lessons-learned documents.

**Open (published, with attached licence terms):**

The detection-pattern catalog itself, in `checks/`, under Apache 2.0
(matching the existing Preston-Check licence). The methodology
specification, under CC-BY 4.0 with retained trademark. The
attestation log, under CC-BY 4.0 to encourage citation. The
adversarial test sets used for sandbox red-teaming, under CC-BY 4.0
to encourage academic study. Embargoed older corpus snapshots
(twelve-month delay), under CC-BY-SA 4.0. The verification
specification (the protocol any third party uses to verify an
attestation), under MIT-0 to maximise adoption.

This split is intentional. Anything that would compound competitive
advantage if disclosed is closed. Anything that would compound
community trust, citations, or regulatory adoption if disclosed is
open. The two sets are non-overlapping; the open material is
operationally useless without the closed material, but the closed
material's value depends entirely on the open material providing
distribution and credibility.

## Licensing strategy

Three licensing channels are realistic and worth structuring:

The first is corpus licensing for non-Preston-Check use cases. Cyber-
insurance underwriters pricing fintech risk, academic security
researchers doing large-scale empirical studies, and LLM providers
training security-tuned models all have legitimate commercial demand
for the labelled positive/negative corpora. Offer the rolling-current
snapshot under a paid commercial licence with clear non-redistribution
terms; offer the embargoed older snapshots free under CC-BY-SA. Pricing
target: USD $25,000–$100,000 per annual subscription, depending on
licensee size and use case.

The second is methodology certification. Once the methodology spec is
published and the trademark is registered, vertical-specific
implementations (medical device, automotive, ICS) can license the
trademark to claim conformance. Pricing target: a small upfront fee
plus a percentage royalty on revenue derived from the certified
implementation. Conformance is verified by submitting attestations
through Preston-Check's verification endpoint, which makes
certification a low-cost-to-deliver service.

The third is the attestation index as a regulatory data feed. Once the
index is cited in compliance frameworks, regulatory bodies and
audit firms will need programmatic access to the live data feed.
Offer access under a commercial API licence, similar to how MITRE
licences CVE feeds or how SPDX licenses package metadata. Pricing
target: tiered by query volume.

All three licensing channels reinforce the closed-current-and-open-
embargoed split: the value of the licence is access to the *current*
state, which is the part that is closed.

## Defensive vs offensive IP posture

The recommended posture is purely defensive. Specifically:

Patents are filed to prevent competitors from patenting the same
methods and asserting against us, and to provide bargaining chips in
the event of a hostile patent assertion against the operating LLC.
They are *not* used to extract licence revenue from third parties or
to suppress competitive products. This posture is publicly committed
to via an Open-Invention-Network-style pledge.

Trade secrets are protected through standard legal mechanisms
(written confidentiality policies, contractor and employee NDAs, KMS-
encrypted storage, internal classification) but are not enforced
through aggressive litigation. If a trade secret is independently re-
derived by a competitor, that is a market signal that the trade
secret has decayed and the focus should shift to the next layer of
the moat.

Trademarks are enforced rigorously, because dilution is irreversible
once it occurs. The methodology trademark in particular must be
defended against unlicensed conformance claims; otherwise the
licensing channel collapses. Standard trademark watch services and
takedown procedures apply.

Copyright is enforced selectively — against verbatim copying of the
specification text, the corpus structure, or the attestation log
schema, but not against derivative works or competing implementations
that are clean-room developed.

## Recommended filing sequence and timing

Phase one (months zero to three, before the first public commit of
the auto-merge code): file provisional patent applications on all
five candidate inventions. Cost: approximately USD $1,500 in filing
fees plus $5,000–$15,000 in attorney drafting per provisional. Total
phase-one cost: $35,000–$80,000. This phase must complete before the
first public disclosure that would trigger absolute-novelty bars in
non-US jurisdictions.

Phase two (months three to nine): register trademarks on the four
distinctive marks (corpus name, methodology name, attestation index
name, detection-time-benchmark name). USPTO filings at $250–$350 per
class per mark, totalling roughly $5,000 with attorney fees. Madrid
Protocol extension to EU and UK at filing-time saves approximately
forty percent versus separate filings later. Total phase-two cost:
$15,000–$25,000.

Phase three (months nine to twelve): publish the methodology
specification and the verification specification under the licences
described above. Begin the corpus-licensing channel with a small
number of pilot licensees. File copyright on the database structure
and the attestation schema. Convert provisional patent filings to
non-provisional and file PCT applications for the highest-priority
inventions (probably the adversarial-loop and the quorum-gated
telemetry methods first). Total phase-three cost: $40,000–$80,000.

Phase four (months twelve onward): patent prosecution, ongoing
trademark watch, gradually-expanding corpus licensing, methodology
certification programme launch, attestation-index commercial-feed
launch. Annual operating cost: $50,000–$100,000 sustaining.

Cumulative budget through patent grant on all five inventions:
$200,000–$350,000 over four years, with revenue-positive crossover
expected in year two from corpus licensing and certification
revenue.

## Risks to the IP and mitigations

Independent re-derivation of the trade-secret components by a
sufficiently-funded competitor. Mitigation: the patent portfolio
covers the genuinely novel methods, so re-derivation produces an
infringing product even if the trade secret was rediscovered. The
methodology spec and the attestation log create prior art that
defines the field around our terminology, making re-derivation
without using our terminology extremely difficult.

Patent invalidity discovered during prosecution. Mitigation: thorough
prior-art search before each provisional filing. Engage a patent
attorney with security-tooling experience specifically; do not use a
generalist. Be prepared to abandon any claim where strong prior art
emerges and consolidate effort on the remaining claims.

Trademark genericisation through over-success. If "VAC-compliant"
becomes a generic term for any verification-walled security catalog
automation, the trademark is dead. Mitigation: enforce the mark
consistently from day one, always use it as an adjective ("VAC-
compliant implementation," never "a VAC"), and require licensees to
follow the same usage rules.

Regulatory frameworks adopting our methodology under a different
name. Mitigation: actively engage with NIST, ENISA, and similar
bodies during methodology spec development; offer the spec for
inclusion-by-reference in standards rather than allowing it to be
re-derived. The reference inclusion preserves trademark and origin
attribution.

Pseudonymity collapse exposing the operator's real identity in
filings. Mitigation: all filings go through the operating LLC, with
the LLC itself owned through a trust or holding entity that obscures
the natural person. Patent inventorship must list real natural-
person inventors by law in most jurisdictions, so the inventor
listing is the weak point; consult counsel on whether co-inventors
or legal-entity-only assignment is acceptable in target jurisdictions.

Slow regulatory adoption rendering the citation moat thin. Mitigation:
seed the citation moat aggressively in year one through academic
collaborations, conference talks under the Preston X persona, and
contributions to open compliance frameworks (PCI DSS, DORA, NIST
SSDF). The citation moat compounds slowly; early seeding is
disproportionately valuable.

## Open questions for the operator and counsel

Several decisions exceed the scope of this strategy document and
require operator judgment, often with legal input:

The choice of operating LLC jurisdiction (Wyoming is mentioned in
the standalone-identity strategy memo, but Delaware, Nevada, and
several non-US options have different IP-holding implications).

The choice of how to structure the patent inventorship while
preserving operator pseudonymity, given that most patent regimes
require named natural-person inventors.

The decision of whether to join the Open Invention Network formally
or to draft a custom non-aggression pledge that fits Preston-Check's
specific scope.

The pricing tiers for corpus licensing, methodology certification,
and attestation-index commercial feeds — these depend on go-to-
market intelligence the operator has and this document does not.

The choice of patent-prosecution counsel — preferring counsel with
direct experience in security-tooling patents, ideally having
prosecuted patents around fuzzing, static analysis, or similar
adjacent fields.

## Summary

The intellectual property generated by the auto-evolving threat-intel
pipeline is unusually rich for a single product because the system
produces durable artefacts as a side-effect of running. Across six
distinct categories — process flywheel, reproducible corpora,
attestation log, methodology specification, defensive patents, and
time-to-detection benchmark — Preston-Check accumulates moats that
are hard to clone, expensive to compete with, and defensible through
established legal mechanisms. The recommended path is a phased filing
sequence over the first twelve months that locks in priority dates
before public disclosure, followed by ongoing prosecution and
licensing programme development through year four. Total cumulative
investment of roughly USD $200,000–$350,000 over four years yields
an IP portfolio valued at several multiples of cost based on
comparable security-tooling acquisitions, while preserving the open-
core distribution model that gives the scanner its market position.

Filings should go through the operating LLC to preserve the
standalone-identity strategy and to provide a clean legal entity for
licensing transactions. All claims in this document should be
reviewed by registered patent counsel before any filing decision is
made.
